What Are Veeners?

Veneers are thin shells, often made of porcelain, that are bonded to the front of teeth to change their color, shape, length, or spacing. The dentist takes a thin layer off the front of the tooth, makes a mold, and fits a shell that matches the teeth around it. They are not the same as a crown, which covers the whole tooth, and they are not the same as whitening, which only changes shade. A veneer can fix a chip, close a small gap, and even out teeth that look uneven. At Steven Weiss DDS, the team shows you shade samples and talks about how many teeth to treat, so the result looks natural, not like a row of fake teeth.

Who Is a Good Fit for Them

Veneers suit people whose teeth are healthy but look stained, chipped, worn, or uneven. They suit people who want a change that lasts longer than whitening, and who are ready to care for the shells with brushing and flossing. They are not the best fit for teeth with large decay, gum trouble, or a habit of grinding hard at night, since those can break a shell. The team will check your teeth and gums first, and they will tell you straight if a veneer, bonding, or whitening fits you best. The Veener Steps, Visit by Visit

First comes a talk and a look at your smile, with photos and a plan for which teeth to treat. Next, the team shapes the front of each tooth a tiny bit, takes a mold, and often places a temporary shell while the real ones are made. At the next visit, they try the shells on, check the shade and the bite, and bond them in place. You will be asked to bite, smile, and speak, so the team can check that the shells feel right. A Veeners dentist Palm Beach Gardens visit for this work usually takes two or three visits in total, and the team tells you that up front, with costs, so there are no surprises.

How to Keep Them Looking Good

After veneers are placed, brush and floss as you would with natural teeth, and come in for regular cleanings. Skip biting hard things like ice, pens, and hard candy with the front teeth, since that can chip a shell. If you grind your teeth at night, ask about a night guard. Coffee, tea, and red wine can stain the edges over time, so rinse with water after them when you can. With good care, veneers can last for years. If one chips or comes loose, call the office and do not try to glue it back yourself. Frequently Asked Questions

How long do veneers last?

With good brushing, flossing, and regular checkups, veneers can last for years, often a decade or more. Avoid biting hard things with your front teeth, and ask about a night guard if you grind your teeth at night.

Do veneers hurt to get?

Most people say the visits are calm, because the tooth is numbed when the front is shaped a tiny bit. You may feel sore for a day or two after, and the team will tell you what care helps most.

Can veneers fix a gap between my front teeth?

Yes, a small gap can often be closed with veneers, or with bonding if the gap is tiny. The team will look at your bite and your teeth, and they will tell you which choice looks most natural for your smile.

Do veneers look fake?

They should not, when they are made to match your other teeth in shade, shape, and size. The team shows you shade samples, tries the shells on, and checks your smile in normal light before they bond them in place.

What is the difference between veneers and bonding?

Bonding uses a tooth-colored resin in one visit to fix a small chip or gap, while veneers are thin shells made in a lab that cover the whole front of a tooth. Bonding costs less up front, while veneers often last longer and resist stains better.

Can I whiten my teeth after veneers?

Whitening does not change the shade of a veneer, so it is best to whiten your natural teeth first, then pick a veneer shade that matches. Tell the team if you want whiter teeth, so they can plan the order of care with you.
